2026 RAP RULES · EXACT MATH

Earning $50,000? Your federal student loan payment is $83.33/month.

Under the Repayment Assistance Plan that replaced SAVE on July 1, 2026, your payment depends on your income — not your balance. Here's the tier-by-tier math, and the cases where that's bad news.

Each dependent child subtracts $50/month: one child → $33.33/mo; two or more children → the $10/mo statutory floor.

The catch at $50k: old plans were cheaper. Under the old IBR (still available to grandfathered borrowers), $50k income with a family of four often meant a $0 payment because of the poverty-line exemption RAP eliminated. If you're on old IBR, run both plans below before switching — moving to RAP could raise your payment, and switching back may not be allowed.

$50k salary: RAP vs Standard at different balances

At $83.33/mo the payment never covers 6.5% interest, so the balance grows for 30 years — even at a $20k balance. This negative-amortization bomb is exactly why low-payment + high-balance borrowers should compare Standard or PSLF seriously, and why forgiveness being taxable since Jan 1, 2026 changes everything.
