Nurses: the 2026 loan rules changed your best strategy

Median RN pay is ~$90k, but BSN/MSN debt of $60k–$150k is common — and most nurses work at nonprofit hospital systems, which makes you PSLF-eligible. That combination is the single most valuable position under the new rules, if you don't break it.

The three things every nurse must get right in 2026

  • PSLF still works, and RAP counts. The 120-payment structure survived the new law. Your RAP payments count toward forgiveness, and PSLF forgiveness is still tax-free — unlike RAP's own 30-year forgiveness, which is now taxable.
  • Never pick Tiered Standard. It's the new non-income-based option your servicer may steer you toward — payments don't count toward PSLF. Ten years of payments, zero progress.
  • Certify employment now. The buyback formula for SAVE-era forbearance periods got more expensive on March 31, 2026. Lock in your payment count with an employment certification before rules shift again.
Typical outcome (calculator below is pre-set to it): An RN with $85,000 in loans at 6% and a $72,000 salary pays roughly $187/month under RAP — and after 120 payments at a qualifying nonprofit, walks away with ~$124,000 forgiven, tax-free. On Standard 10-year she'd pay ~$944/month and finish with nothing forgiven. Adjust the numbers to your own situation.

Nurse loan FAQs

Do travel-nursing agencies count for PSLF?

Only if the agency itself is a qualifying government or 501(c)(3) employer. Most for-profit staffing agencies do not count — check your employer's status on the PSLF Help Tool before signing a contract.

Should I refinance my federal loans?

If you're pursuing PSLF: no, never. Refinancing to a private loan permanently destroys PSLF eligibility. Only refinance if you've abandoned forgiveness entirely and rates beat your current APR.

What about nurse-specific state repayment programs?

Many states run loan-repayment programs for nurses in underserved areas (often $10k–$40k over 2–4 years). These usually stack with PSLF. Search "[your state] nurse loan repayment program" — this varies too much to generalize.
