Your SAVE plan ended. Here's what happens if you do nothing.

On July 1, 2026 the SAVE plan was eliminated. Roughly 9 million borrowers must now choose a new plan — and the default option is the most expensive one.

The trap: silence = Standard repayment After your servicer notifies you, you have 90 days to act. If you don't choose, you're auto-moved to the Standard 10-year plan. For a $45,000 balance at 6.5%, that's $511/month — versus roughly $104/month under RAP at a $55,000 income. Missing the higher payments puts you on the road to default, where the government can garnish wages and seize tax refunds. A quarter of all borrowers were already delinquent in 2025.

Your options, ranked by what they cost

Switch to RAP (the new income-driven plan) — usually the lowest payment

1%–10% of your AGI in marginal tiers, minus $50/month per child, $10 floor. Forgiveness after 30 years — but the forgiven amount is now taxable. Best if you need cash-flow relief today.

Stay on (or move to) old IBR if you're grandfathered

Existing borrowers can keep IBR, which still has the 20/25-year forgiveness and the old poverty-line-based formula. For low income + high debt, old IBR can beat RAP. Compare both before switching — switching may be irreversible.

PSLF workers: get on RAP and certify your employment

RAP counts toward the 120 payments and PSLF forgiveness is still tax-free. Submit your employment certification now to lock in credit for payments already made. Do NOT pick Tiered Standard — it doesn't count toward PSLF.

Quick answers

When exactly did SAVE end?

July 1, 2026. SAVE had already been blocked by courts since 2024, with most borrowers parked in interest-free forbearance. The 2025 law (P.L. 119-21) formally eliminated it and created RAP as its replacement.

Do my past SAVE payments still count for PSLF?

Payments already certified are protected. But the "buyback" formula for counting forbearance periods since July 18, 2024 was revised on March 31, 2026 and now costs more. Certify your employment immediately to preserve your count.

What if I'm already in default?

Defaulted borrowers have separate exit routes (fresh-start style rehabilitation programs are being rolled back — check your servicer). Get out of default before plan selection matters, because collections continue regardless of which plan you pick.
