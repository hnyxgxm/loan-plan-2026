The 2026 rules, in plain English

What is the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)?

From July 1, 2026, RAP is the only income-driven plan for new federal borrowers. Your payment is built from marginal tiers of your AGI: 1% of income between $10k–$20k, 2% between $20k–$30k, and so on up to 10% of income above $100k. Each dependent child subtracts $50/month; the floor is $10/month. Whatever balance remains after 30 years of qualifying payments is forgiven.

Is the forgiven amount taxable?

Yes — this is the trap. The federal tax exclusion for IDR forgiveness expired, and since January 1, 2026 forgiven balances are taxable income. A borrower with $80k forgiven could owe ~$20k+ in tax in the year of forgiveness. PSLF forgiveness remains tax-free, which is why the PSLF track wins for public-service workers. Some states also tax the forgiven amount; our calculator estimates both.

What happened to SAVE, IBR, PAYE and ICR?

SAVE ended July 1, 2026. If you were enrolled, your servicer notifies you and you have 90 days to choose a new plan — otherwise you are auto-moved to Standard repayment (often 3–5× the RAP payment). Old IBR remains available to existing borrowers; PAYE and ICR sunset by July 1, 2028. New borrowers cannot get any of them.

Does PSLF still exist?

Yes. The 120-payment / 10-year structure survives, RAP payments count, and PSLF forgiveness stays tax-free. But the new "Tiered Standard" plan does not count toward PSLF — picking the wrong plan can waste years of progress. See our guide for nurses for the PSLF-specific math.
